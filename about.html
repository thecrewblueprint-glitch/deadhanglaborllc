The person behind the LLC

Deadhang Labor is an owner-operated production labor company built from actual calls: unloads, builds, department support, show positions, strikes, travel days, and the long hours between them.

Aaron Bowman entered live-event production through hands-on field work and developed across staging, structures, lighting, video, audio support, load-in/load-out, and spotlight assignments. The operating philosophy is simple: be useful, respect the lead, understand the boundary of the assignment, communicate clearly, and keep learning.

The company is not positioned as the producer, designer, or engineering authority behind the shows in its portfolio. Deadhang Labor documents the labor/support role it actually performed inside those larger production systems.

Working profile

How the work developed.

FIELD / 01

General stagehand foundation

Load-in/load-out, case movement, staging support, cable and department assistance, venue workflow, and learning the pace and chain of command of active calls.

FIELD / 02

Structures & systems exposure

Hands-on work around G2, Tait, PGP Blue Steel, Layher scaffolding, large-format video, lighting systems, scenic elements, barricade, and other production infrastructure under appropriate direction.

FIELD / 03

Arena & stadium production

Load-ins and production support at large venues including State Farm Stadium, Mortgage Matchup Center, Desert Diamond Arena, Chase Field, and Northern Arizona's Skydome environment.

FIELD / 04

Festival travel

Work expanded beyond Arizona into multi-day and travel-based festival environments including Electric Forest, Lost Lands, Country Thunder, and other large outdoor productions.

FIELD / 05

Show-call responsibility

Assigned spotlight work in arena and festival environments, with emphasis on preparation, cues, framing, performer tracking, communication, and safe operation from elevated positions.

Direction

More capable every call.

The longer-range goal is not to inflate the current business into something it is not. It is to keep building field competence, stronger systems understanding, and eventually the ability to support larger scopes and qualified crews with the same discipline expected on today's calls.
