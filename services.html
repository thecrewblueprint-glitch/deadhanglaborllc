Where Deadhang fits.

This page is intentionally written as a scope sheet rather than a sales menu. Actual assignments are performed within the production's chain of command, site rules, training limits, and qualified-person boundaries.

01

Staging & structural support

Ground-support builds, structural assembly, teardown, decks, barricade, scenic elements, and related production structures under qualified direction and site-specific requirements.

Worked around: G2 / Tait / PGP Blue Steel / Layher
02

Load-in / load-out

Truck unload and reload, road-case movement, department sorting, staging components, equipment positioning, strike support, and other physical workflows required to move a production into and out of a venue or festival site.

Arena / stadium / festival / corporate
03

Department support

General labor assistance for lighting, video, audio, staging, scenic, and production teams, including cable runs, equipment movement, case management, truss assistance, video-wall panel handling, and other tasks as directed.

Lighting / video / audio / staging / scenic
04

Spotlight operations

Show-call spotlight support as trained, assigned, and authorized, including system preparation, cue execution, performer tracking, framing, focus, and communication with the lighting team.

Arena / festival / elevated positions
05

Travel-based production

Festival runs, extended calls, and other U.S. travel assignments when scope, travel, lodging, meals/per diem, and other practical arrangements are agreed in advance.

Multi-day / travel / festival runs
06

Corporate & private production

Production labor for corporate events, private shows, branded experiences, and venue conversions where the engagement length, local availability, and required scope fit the business's operating model.

One-week minimum generally preferred

What the wording means.

Does “structural support” mean engineering or rigging authority?

No. It describes labor participation under the direction, qualifications, site rules, and system requirements applicable to the call. The portfolio does not claim design, engineering, inspection, or supervisory authority that was not actually held.

Do you travel?

Yes. U.S. travel work is considered when the production dates and practical travel/accommodation arrangements are agreed before the assignment.

What types of calls fit best?

Festival runs, arena and stadium load-ins, multi-day event commitments, touring support, assigned spotlight positions, and longer production engagements are the strongest fit.

What departments have you supported?

Audio, video, lighting, staging, scenic, and general production. The exact task scope depends on the call and the direction of the department lead.

What should an inquiry include?

Dates, city/venue, event type, expected department or role, call duration, travel arrangement if applicable, and the best production contact.

How quickly do you respond?

Typically within 24–48 hours. A detailed inquiry is easier to evaluate quickly than a generic availability message.
